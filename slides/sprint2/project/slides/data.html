Where it comes from

The data contract in the group binder (P01 to P07), generated with a fixed seed. Layla Hassan and OP-028 are kept exactly as in the binder, with the eight data problems we test against.

Every person and company is invented. No real student data.

Missing or uncertain, on purpose

Dates left empty show as “cannot confirm”, never “no”. Skills a student lists are “unverified” until training checks them. Drafts newer than the submitted profile are ignored. 37 students wrote 23 skills in their own words.

Who may change what

Everyone reads everything. Each team writes only its own tables, with its own key; versions are added, never overwritten; nobody deletes. A sandbox lets teams practise.

Team 5, P05 Opportunity Matcher

3

Our sample data now lives in one shared Supabase database that all seven teams can use: 400 students with 808 profile versions, 240 internships from 60 employers, 178 of them open, a shared list of 44 skill terms, 616 applications and over 8,000 stored match results. Everything is synthetic and follows the data contract in the binder; Layla and the Emirates aviation job are kept exactly as in the binder, together with the eight data problems we test against. We kept missing and uncertain information on purpose, because real data is messy: empty dates show as “cannot confirm”, skills a student lists are unverified until training checks them, and free-text skills stay unrecognised until a person confirms a mapping. Access is simple: everyone reads, each team writes only its own tables with its own key, versions are only ever added, and nobody can delete. This replaces Sprint 1’s private demo file.
